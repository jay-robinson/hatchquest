Privacy Policy

Effective 18 November 2026.

The short version, for parents

We don't collect anything about your child, or about you. HatchQuest has no accounts, no sign-in and no server. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and the app has no code from other companies.

What the app keeps stays on the device. Your child's dragon, progress and settings are saved on the iPhone or iPad the app is installed on. None of it is sent to us.

No real names. A grown-up picks a two-word player name from the app's lists. There's no keyboard or text box anywhere in the app, so nobody types a name or a message.

Money is Apple's. The membership is sold and handled by Apple. We never see your card, your name or your Apple Account.

Grown-ups only. Purchases, settings for grown-ups and every link out of the app sit behind a grown-up gate. Children don't see prices.

1. Who this covers

This policy covers the HatchQuest app for iPhone and iPad, and this website. HatchQuest is a math practice game for children from kindergarten to grade 8 and the grown-ups who look after them. It's in the App Store's Education category, not the Kids Category. We follow the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). Because we collect no personal information from anyone, there is very little for us to do under it, and this page says plainly what the app does and doesn't do.

2. What we collect

Nothing. We don't receive, store or have access to any personal information, usage data, device identifier, location, contact details, purchase details or crash reports from the app. The app has no way to send any of that to us: it has no account system, no server of ours and no analytics or advertising code.

Apple, apart from the app, may show us crash reports and totals such as how many devices opened the app, but only from people who chose to share analytics with app developers in their device's settings. These don't name you or your child.

The app doesn't ask to use the camera, microphone, photos, contacts or location, and it never shows a tracking prompt.

3. What the app keeps on the device

To work, the app saves this on the device where it's installed:

  • For each player: the picked two-word name, the egg and dragon, how much it has grown, coins, how far each run got, and which questions were answered right or wrong (the app uses this to choose the next questions and to show the grown-ups page).
  • The grade and the month school starts, chosen by a grown-up, and settings such as sound, music and text size.
  • Whether the family is a member, as reported by Apple, so the right worlds open, even offline.
  • A player a grown-up removes, for 30 days, so it can be brought back. After that it's gone.
  • A friend's visiting dragon, for one day (see Dragon visits, below).
  • A random id the app makes up for this device to keep its saves in order. It isn't read from the device and never leaves it.

This stays on the device. It isn't sent to us or to anyone else. In this version the save isn't copied to iCloud or to your other devices.

4. Purchases and the membership

HatchQuest is free to download, and the first 100 lanes of Thornkeep are free for good. An auto-renewing membership, sold through the App Store, opens the other worlds: $3.99 a month or $24.99 a year in the United States. Prices come from the App Store and can differ by country.

  • Apple takes the payment, handles your card and your Apple Account, and sends the receipts. We never see or store any of it.
  • Apple tells the app on your device whether your family is a member. That check is between your device and Apple.
  • Apple gives us sales totals, such as how many memberships were sold in a country. They don't name you or your child.
  • To cancel, use Apple's subscription settings: on iPhone or iPad, open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions.

5. The grown-up gate

Purchases, the grown-ups page and every link out of the app are behind a gate: hold a button for three seconds, then type the year you were born on a number pad. The year is checked on the device and thrown away; it isn't saved or sent anywhere. It keeps young children away from purchases and links.

6. Dragon visits

A child can show their dragon to a friend. A visit carries only the dragon's kind and how big it has grown, and its picked name only if a grown-up turns that on. The friend can tap in six pictures read off the screen.

If a grown-up turns on "Find nearby devices", two devices in the same room can also find each other over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, never over the internet, but only while the Side by side screen is open. While it's open, the device shows other HatchQuest devices nearby the dragon's kind and size, the same as its six pictures do, under the name "dragon", never the device's own name. The other device asks before a visit comes in, and only after a yes does the picked name, if it's on, go across, encrypted. Turning on "Find nearby devices" is when the app asks to use your local network.

A visiting dragon stays with the player it visits for one day, then the app forgets it. Nothing in a visit reaches us, and nothing is kept about the other device. Visits, nearby devices and the name each have a switch on the grown-ups page.

7. Links to other sites

Behind the gate, the app links to this policy, the terms of use (Apple's standard license agreement) and Apple's subscription page. Those open in the system browser or Apple's own sheet, under each site's own policy. The app doesn't load web pages inside itself.

8. Advertising and tracking

There are no ads. We don't track anyone across apps or websites. We don't sell or share information, because we don't have any.

9. Children

HatchQuest is made for children, and it's built so we collect no personal information from them. We don't ask for it, a child can't type free text, and there's no way for a child to talk to other players. A dragon visit carries no messages, and the picked name only if a grown-up turns that on. If you believe a child's personal information has reached us anyway, contact us (below) and we'll delete it.

10. Deleting data

Everything the app keeps is on the device. Deleting the app from the device deletes all of it. Because we hold nothing ourselves, there's nothing for us to delete.

11. Security

Only two things ever leave the device: a nearby dragon visit, if a grown-up turns that on, which goes straight to a device in the same room (the name, if it's on, encrypted), and the membership check, which is between the device and Apple. There's no server of ours for anyone to break into.

12. Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes what it does with information, we'll update this page and its date before the new version goes out. We won't start collecting personal information from children without a parent's verifiable consent first, as COPPA requires.
